Mathematical Model Formulation (Linear Programming)

The blending problem is modeled as a continuous Linear Program with 16 decision variables and 12 constraints.

1. Sets & Indices:
i ∈ Feedstocks = { Alkylate, CCG, SRG, Isopentane }
j ∈ Gasolines = { Gas A, Gas B, Gas C }
2. Decision Variables:
xij ≥ 0 : Thousands of gallons of feedstock i used to blend gasoline j
si ≥ 0 : Thousands of gallons of unblended leftover feedstock i sold at residual market value
3. Objective Function (Maximize Total Gross Revenue):
max Z = ∑j pj (∑i xij) + ∑i vi si
Where pA = $3.00, pB = $3.50, pC = $4.00; v = [ $4.50, $2.50, $2.25, $2.35 ]
4. Constraints:
• Feedstock Supply Balance: ∑j xij + si = Supplyi, ∀ i ∈ Feedstocks
• Contractual Minimum Demand: ∑i xij ≥ Demandj, ∀ j ∈ {A, B, C}
• Max Reid Vapor Pressure (RVP ≤ 7.0 psi): ∑i (RVPi - 7.0) xij ≤ 0, ∀ j
• Min Octane Rating:
   • Gas A (Low TEL): ∑i (OctaneLowi - 90) xiA ≥ 0
   • Gas B (High TEL): ∑i (OctaneHighi - 97) xiB ≥ 0
   • Gas C (High TEL): ∑i (OctaneHighi - 100) xiC ≥ 0
• Company Policy Side Constraint: ∑i xiA ≥ ∑i xiB (Gas A ≥ Gas B)

Strategic Scenario Evaluations (Case Questions 1 to 5)

Question 1: Economic Cost of the Side Constraint (Gas A ≥ Gas B)

Finding: Enforcing Gas A ≥ Gas B costs Jansen Gas $6,988.41. Removing this constraint raises revenue from $1,718,021.80 to $1,725,010.21.

Production shifts: Gas A drops from 130k to its contract floor of 120k gal, while high-margin Gas B surges from 130k to 194.69k gal.

Question 2: Technical Viability of Medium TEL for Gas B

Finding: Strictly Infeasible

Under Medium TEL, achieving 97 octane requires massive amounts of Alkylate and Isopentane. However, Isopentane's RVP of 20 breaches the 7.0 psi vapor pressure cap, and Alkylate is simultaneously needed by Gas C (100 octane). The specifications cannot be co-satisfied.

Question 3: Stricter Reid Vapor Pressure Standards

Finding: The maximum allowable RVP can be lowered from 7.0 psi down to 6.43 psi before blending becomes impossible. Each 0.1 psi reduction costs ~$10,000 in lost revenue as Isopentane is forced into low-value salvage.

Question 4: Gas A Maximum Feasible Octane Frontier

Finding: Gas A's minimum octane requirement can be raised from 90.0 up to 91.80. Beyond 91.80, the model becomes infeasible. Raising octane to 91.0 reduces revenue by -$28,950 because Alkylate is diverted away from $4.00 Gas C.

Question 5: Proportional Price and Cost Invariance

Finding: Dave Wagner is 100% Correct

If all gas prices and feedstock residual values scale uniformly by +10% (or shift by a constant +$1/gal), the optimal blending decision variables remain 100.0% identical. Blending decisions depend purely on relative economic tradeoffs.
